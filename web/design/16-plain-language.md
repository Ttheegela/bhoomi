# 16 Plain-language layer (rework PR6): three short lines on the page, in Ask, in voice and from MCP

Owner's decision (fixed): every answer leads with three short lines: (1) what's expected, (2) how sure the experts are in everyday words, (3) what it means day to day. Tarun: "how would a general audience would understand how would these numbers mean for them". Data: `docs/CONTRACTS.md` "Sea-level expert projection (PR2)", "CMIP6 expert projection (PR3)", "Facts, not advice". This file extends `11`, `14` and `15` and wins where they differ.

Pictures (design mock, "sample": the current page from the worktree, API answered from `tests/fixtures/rework`, so observed values are made up and projection values are the real AR6 and CMIP6 packs): `rework-pr6-plain-design/` in the side-session notes. `templates-check.py` there renders §1 from the fixtures; every example string below is its output.

## 0. Rules for every line
1. **The stats lane writes the lines; nobody else builds or edits them.** Page, Ask, voice and MCP print them verbatim, so the invented-numbers guard passes (every number is in a tool-result string).
2. **Record-free.** With a projection, the lines use only the projection (and its own caveat). No `now`, no `baseline.value`, no verdict, no `check`: they read the same whichever record is main (ERA5 for temperature and hottest day after PR4, POWER for rain, CO-OPS or PSMSL for sea level) and whichever source the visitor picks. The past belongs to the verdict, `fact` and Ask's own text.
3. **Facts, not advice.** No "you should", no buy, build, move, avoid or insure, no risk word. "Would" only for what follows from the projected number itself (high tides ride on mean sea level).
4. **Spoken form.** Years "1995 to 2014", "2041 to 2060", never a dash. No brackets, no signs: the direction word carries the sign ("36 cm higher", "3% less"). No-break space before "cm", "mm", "°C".
5. **The right range name.** AR6 sea level, medium confidence: "about a 2 in 3 chance" (its likely range, 17th to 83rd percentile), always "on that path". CMIP6 10th to 90th percentile: "8 in 10 climate models put it …", followed by "That shows how much the models agree, not how likely it is." Never "chance" or "odds" for CMIP6, and "likely" only inside that fixed sentence; never the idiom "not a chance".
6. **Numbers** use the server's own rounding: `sig2` for single values, `ends()` for a range (both ends to the same decimals, as `readouts.by_2050.text`), so the lines match R3's old values and the chart readout exactly.

## 1. The templates (`S` = a scenario row; sea level at 2050 (`i` = index of 2050 in `p.years`), CMIP6 at 2041–2060 (`w` = 1))
Slots: `{v}` `sig2(S.p50)` unsigned with its unit (sea level mm ÷ 10 → cm); `{range}` = "between {a} and {b}{u} {up}" when both ends ≥ 0, "between {|b|} and {|a|}{u} {down}" when both ≤ 0, "between {|a|}{u} {down} and {b}{u} {up}" across 0 (rain "between 3% less and 24% more rain"); an end that rounds to 0 is "no change". `{up}/{down}`: sea higher/lower; air temperature warmer/cooler; hottest day hotter/cooler; rain "more"/"less" in line 1, "more rain"/"less rain" in ranges. `{path}` = "the {S.name} path" ("the intermediate emissions path").

| | Line 1, what's expected | Line 2, how sure | Line 3, day to day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sea level, medium confidence | "By 2050, experts project the sea here about {v} {up\|down} than its 1995 to 2014 average, on {path}." | "Experts give about a 2 in 3 chance it lands {range(S.p17, S.p83)} on that path. {p.caveat}" | "So each high tide would come in about that much {up\|down} than in 1995 to 2014, and the sea would still be rising about {sig2(S.rate.p50)} mm a year." (rate ≤ 0 or null: end after "1995 to 2014.") |
| Sea level, low confidence (`_low`) | as above | "Experts have low confidence in this path: it adds ice-sheet loss that is hard to predict, so they give no odds for it. Its middle range is {range(S.p17, S.p83)}." | as above |
| Air temperature (CMIP6) | "Around 2050, climate models put the average year here about {v} {warmer\|cooler} than in 1995 to 2014, on {path}." | "8 in 10 climate models put it {range(S.p10, S.p90)}. That shows how much the models agree, not how likely it is." | "An average day, summer and winter together, would be about that much {warmer\|cooler}; this does not say how summers alone would change." |
| Hottest day (CMIP6) | "Around 2050, climate models put each month's hottest day here about {v} {hotter\|cooler} than in 1995 to 2014, on {path}." | as air temperature | "So in a typical month the hottest day would be about that much {hotter\|cooler}; the year's single hottest day is not projected here." |
| Rain (CMIP6), models agree | "Around 2050, climate models put a year's rain here about {v} {more\|less} than in 1995 to 2014, on {path}." | as air temperature | "For every 100 mm of rain a usual year brought here in 1995 to 2014, a year around 2050 would bring about {100 + sig2(S.p50)} mm." (a whole number: 111, 109, 95) |
| Rain, `S.disagree[w]` true | as above | "The models disagree on wetter or drier: 8 in 10 of them put it {range(S.p10, S.p90)}." | as above |

- `{v}` rounds to 0: line 1 says "about the same as in 1995 to 2014" (sea "at about its 1995 to 2014 average"), and line 3 is left out.
- `S`'s value is null at that year: one line only, "By 2050, the experts give no value here on {path}." / "Around 2050, the climate models have no value here on {path}."
- **No projection** (`p.available` false: water storage, a record with no 1995–2014 average, the Great Lakes, a cell with no value): `[p.why, {how}, meaning.comparison]`, where `{how}` by `meaning.confidence` is the prompt's rule-D sentence: "Very clear" "A change this steady would be very unlikely if nothing were really changing." / "Clear" "A change like this would be unlikely if nothing were really changing." / "Not clear" "The data do not show a clear change: the ups and downs are too large to tell." An empty `comparison` leaves 2 lines.
- Why these day-to-day framings: high tides ride on mean sea level (a fact about water levels, not damage); "an average day, summer and winter together" is exactly what a yearly mean is and tells the Planner the limit; "for every 100 mm" makes a percentage concrete without a record's mm. Familiar-height comparisons (a 30 cm ruler) and thermometer levels (POWER's or ERA5's 11.9 °C) were tried and dropped: the first needs a reference table with false precision, the second depends on the main record (§0.2).

## 3. The page: an "In plain words" box replaces card R3
```
.where  ·  .verdict  ·  p.say {fact}  ·  p.world
.plain   p.pl-h "In plain words"   ul > li × S.plain   p.pl-src "Future: {source} ↗ · {S.code}{tail}"     NEW
.model   (unchanged)
.reads   R1 Now | R2 Rate          (R3 removed when the box shows)
.next  ·  .acts  ·  p.disc
```
1. **When**: `t.projection.available` and `S.plain` present (`S` = the selected scenario, the same `S` the chart radios and low-confidence box set). Otherwise no box and R3 stays exactly as today (`projection.why`, or an answer cached before PR6). Without a projection the verdict word and `fact` already say the past in plain words; a box would repeat them.
2. **Subtraction**: R3 goes. Its value is line 1, its range is line 2, its "more than today" leaves the card (it stays in the chart's readout line, `#future-read`), its source and scenario go to the box foot, its regional-estimate sentence and `check` sentence go to the foot. Nothing else on the card changes; the box never repeats a past number.
3. **Words**: label "In plain words" (Units imperial: "In plain words (in metric)"). Lines `textContent`, verbatim. Foot (14 px `--ink-2`): "Future: " + link to `p.source.record_url` with text "IPCC AR6 sea level projections ↗" or "IPCC AR6 Atlas, CMIP6 ↗" + " · " + `S.code` in a no-wrap span + tail. Tail: sea ", medium confidence" / ", low confidence", then " · Regional estimate: local sinking may differ." when `location.kind` is "regional estimate", then " · {check.text}" when set; CMIP6 ", {S.models[1]} climate models".
4. **Live**: a radio or the low-confidence checkbox swaps the `li`s in place (no re-render of the card, no focus move); the box is not a live region (the chart's readout line already announces the change).
5. **Markup**: `<section class="plain" aria-labelledby="pl-h"><h3 id="pl-h" class="pl-h">…</h3><ul><li>…</li></ul><p class="pl-src">…</p></section>`, placed after `p.world` (after `p.say` when there is no world line), before `.model` and `.reads`.
6. **Style**: fill `--bg`, radius 8, no border, padding 14 18 12 (card under 520 px: 12 14 10). Label 14 px weight 600 `--ink-2`, 8 px below. Lines 16 px `--ink`, line-height 1.45, 8 px apart, no bullets (`list-style: none`, keep `role="list"` for Safari). Foot 10 px above. Margin 0 0 16 px.

## 4. Ask (in-app agent, voice) — order of an answer that looked something up
1. **Plain lines** (code, first, outside the word count, like `[GUIDANCE]`): for each place and measurement in `guidance.picked()`, line 1 is the past clause then `plain[0]`; lines 2 and 3 follow. The past clause is the shared `meaning.headline` of the picked results there, or "Our records here disagree about the past." when their verdicts differ, so it never depends on which record is main. One place and measurement: all lines. Two or more: line 1 of each, at most three, each prefixed "{Place}, {measurement}: ".
2. **The model's text** (≤ 100 words; ≤ 160 for several places or sources): today's B (meaning.comparison), C (figures), D (how sure), E (sources and caveats, adding `location.note` and `check.text` when set; code then adds `projection.models_note` once, in front of `[GUIDANCE]`'s text, or in simple words and voice right after the plain lines, voice as one sentence joined with "; "). It never restates a plain line, and gives no future number of its own (rule F stays). Rule A's first sentence goes: the past clause took its place. The "not only summer" sentence stays for season questions.
3. **`[GUIDANCE]`**: `fact`, "Where to look next", the future link, the disclaimer, unchanged. Then `[OFFICIAL RESOURCES]` for decision questions.
- **"Explain in simple words"** (code detects the phrases "simple words", "simple terms", "explain simply", "explain it simply", "explain that simply", "explain this simply", "say it simply", "plain words", "plain English", "everyday words", "like I'm 10", "for a child", "what does that mean for me", "what does it mean for me", "what does this mean for me"; one list, `prompts.SIMPLE_PHRASES`, which the prompt names too): a follow-up re-calls `detect_trend` for the place it is about (the earlier answer's unless it names another), except a question about what a word means ("What does 95% range mean?", "What is a tide gauge?"; "What does that mean?", "What does this mean for me?" or "What does the trend in Delhi mean?" ask about the earlier answer or a place and do look up). If that one restart brings nothing usable, code answers with the plain lines and the source line; the reply without a lookup is never shown (numbers come only from this turn). The answer is the plain lines, one model sentence naming the sources, then `[GUIDANCE]` without `fact` (it carries "95% range" and the world ratio; contract Ask line amended). No figures, ranges, p-values or caveat lists from the model; ≤ 40 words of its own.
- **Voice**: the ElevenLabs prompt forwards each question with "Explain in simple words." and reads the answer as given, so a listener hears the three lines, where to look next and the disclaimer (coordinator applies; listed in the PR body).

## 5. MCP: `get_projection` (agent lane; `detect_trend`'s description adds: "It also carries plain: two or three short lines for the public. Start with meaning.headline, then plain, word for word.")
Description, exactly: "What experts project for a place: IPCC AR6 sea-level projections at the nearest tide gauge, or IPCC AR6 Atlas (CMIP6) climate-model projections for air temperature, the hottest day of the month and rain. Water storage has none. Start your answer with the strings in `plain`, word for word and in order: what is projected, how sure the experts are, and what it means day to day; they are written for the public and every number in them is in this result. Use the other fields only if the user wants more: typical, likely_range (sea level only: about a 2 in 3 chance, medium confidence), model_range (temperature and rain: how much climate models agree, never a chance or 'likely'), high_end, today_level, scenario. Give details (quantiles, the parts of sea-level rise, provenance) only when asked. Name the source (details.source.name and record_url) and end with disclaimer. These are projections that depend on the world's emissions, not predictions. State no number that is not in this result, and give no advice: no 'you should', no buy, build, move or avoid, no risk level. To say whether the records show a change, use detect_trend."
Inputs: `variable` ("sea_level", "air_temp", "air_temp_max", "precip", "water_storage"), `lat`, `lon`, `scenario?` ("low", "intermediate", "high", "very high", or an id from `details.all_scenarios`; default intermediate), `year?` (default 2050; sea level 2020 to 2150 by tens, CMIP6 2030, 2050 or 2090 for the 20-year window around it), `source?` (as `detect_trend`; only `today_level` and `details.observed_source` depend on it). Errors are `ToolError`s with a plain message (unknown variable or scenario, a year not offered, no tide gauge within 100 km).

Output (types; `value`s in the projection's `units`, `text`s in display units and spoken form):
