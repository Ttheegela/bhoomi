Weather records

Used for air temperature, hottest days and rain. Each answer names the record it used and links to that exact record.

ERA5

A European record of past weather on a grid about 25 km wide.

Open the dataset: ERA5 ↗
Made by
ECMWF, for the Copernicus Climate Change Service
We get it from

Our own copy of the Copernicus dataset ↗ behind the Copernicus Interactive Climate Atlas (its ERA5 part, land only).

If our copy can't be read, or has no land cell for a place, we fetch ERA5 from Open-Meteo instead, and the answer names Open-Meteo.

Licence

CC BY 4.0, the licence of ERA5 ↗

If the answer names Open-Meteo: CC BY 4.0 (Open-Meteo's API data).

Open-Meteo's free service is for non-commercial use only.

Updated
Our copy runs from January 1981 to December 2025. The dataset is updated about once a year; we rebuild our copy by hand.

Credit to give

Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information 2026.

If the answer names Open-Meteo, also: Weather data by Open-Meteo.com ↗

How to cite

Copernicus Climate Change Service, Climate Data Store, (2024): Gridded dataset underpinning the Copernicus Interactive Climate Atlas. Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) Climate Data Store (CDS). DOI: 10.24381/cds.h35hb680 (Accessed on 2026-11-23)

Named in answers: ERA5 reanalysis (Copernicus Interactive Climate Atlas dataset) · ID: doi:10.24381/cds.h35hb680 · Copernicus dataset multi-origin-c3s-atlas, ERA5 part
From Open-Meteo, named in answers: ERA5 reanalysis via Open-Meteo archive · ID: doi:10.24381/cds.adbb2d47 · ERA5 hourly data on single levels from 1940 to present

NASA POWER

A NASA record of past weather on a grid about 50 km wide.

Open the dataset: NASA POWER ↗
Made by
NASA Langley Research Center's POWER project, from the MERRA-2 weather model
We get it from
NASA POWER's monthly data service, for the place's grid cell.
Licence
No licence stated; POWER asks for the credit line, plus the service version and date accessed
Updated
Fetched when an answer needs it: whole months from January 1981 to the end of last year.

Credit to give

The data was obtained from National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) Langley Research Center's Prediction Of Worldwide Energy Resources (POWER) project funded through the NASA Earth Science Division.

  • Keep in mind: NASA POWER comes from a weather model, MERRA-2, which does not use land thermometer readings; a nearby weather station can differ.
  • For rain: NASA POWER's rain is corrected every day to rain-gauge maps. Where gauges are few (much of Africa, inland Australia, South America and the far north), it can drift.
How to cite

POWER also asks for its version and the date you got the data: "The data was obtained from the POWER Project's [version] version on [date]." Step 9 of each answer, "Sources", gives both.

Named in answers: NASA POWER (MERRA-2) · ID: monthly point data, parameters T2M, T2M_MAX and PRECTOTCORR

Weather stations (GHCN-Daily)

Readings from the nearest weather station within 25 km that has at least 30 years of readings since 1981.

Open the dataset: GHCN-Daily ↗
Made by
NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI) ↗, from weather stations worldwide
We get it from
NCEI's data service, day by day. We turn the days into months, leaving out readings that failed NOAA's quality checks.
Licence
No licence stated; NCEI asks for the dataset citation with its version and access date
Updated
Fetched when an answer needs it, through the end of last year.

Credit to give

Menne, M.J., I. Durre, B. Korzeniewski, S. McNeill, K. Thomas, X. Yin, S. Anthony, R. Ray, R.S. Vose, B.E.Gleason, and T.G. Houston, 2012: Global Historical Climatology Network - Daily (GHCN-Daily), Version 3.35. NOAA National Climatic Data Center. http://doi.org/10.7289/V5D21VHZ.

  • Keep in mind: Station records are not adjusted for changes in instruments, observing times or station moves.
How to cite

Menne, M.J., I. Durre, R.S. Vose, B.E. Gleason, and T.G. Houston, 2012: An overview of the Global Historical Climatology Network-Daily Database. Journal of Atmospheric and Oceanic Technology, 29, 897-910, doi:10.1175/JTECH-D-11-00103.1.

Named in answers: NOAA NCEI GHCN-Daily · ID: doi:10.7289/V5D21VHZ · Version 3.35

Sea level

Tide gauges measure the sea against the land at one spot, so land sinking or rising is included. Each answer uses the nearest gauge within 100 km with at least 30 years of monthly readings, the last no more than 5 years before the end of last year.

NOAA tide gauges (CO-OPS)

Readings from the nearest NOAA tide gauge (US coasts).

Open the dataset: NOAA tide gauges ↗
Made by
NOAA's National Ocean Service, Center for Operational Oceanographic Products and Services (CO-OPS)
We get it from
NOAA's Tides and Currents data service: the gauge's verified monthly mean sea level.
Licence
Public domain (US Government information), unless specifically annotated otherwise
Updated
Fetched when an answer needs it, up to the latest month NOAA has verified.

Credit to give

Data from the NOAA National Ocean Service, Center for Operational Oceanographic Products and Services (CO-OPS).

Named in answers: NOAA CO-OPS tide gauges · ID: NOAA CO-OPS verified monthly mean water level (API product monthly_mean)

Tide gauges worldwide (PSMSL)

Readings from the nearest tide gauge in a worldwide collection.

Open the dataset: PSMSL ↗
Made by
Each gauge's own authority; collected and checked by the Permanent Service for Mean Sea Level (PSMSL) ↗
We get it from
PSMSL's monthly files, on each gauge's fixed reference level. We leave out months PSMSL flags as doubtful or filled in.
Licence
No licence stated; PSMSL asks for the data set citation and its latest paper (Holgate et al., 2013)
Updated
Fetched when an answer needs it.

Credit to give

Permanent Service for Mean Sea Level (PSMSL), 2026, "Tide Gauge Data", Retrieved [the "Extracted from Database" date on PSMSL's data page] from http://www.psmsl.org/data/obtaining/.

How to cite

Journal of Coastal Research: Volume 29, Issue 3: pp. 493 – 504. doi:10.2112/JCOASTRES-D-12-00175.1.

Named in answers: Permanent Service for Mean Sea Level (PSMSL), RLR monthly means · ID: PSMSL Revised Local Reference (RLR) monthly mean sea level
